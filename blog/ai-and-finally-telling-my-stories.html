How I Finally Brought These Stories to Life

I could not afford an editor or a cover artist. I also could not afford to keep waiting.

I have been carrying these worlds around for more than forty years.

That is not a figure of speech. As a boy, and later as a man, I used to lie awake at night dreaming them up. Whole places. Whole people. Trains that never arrived where they were supposed to. Towns that felt a little haunted. Stories that would not leave me alone. For most of that time I had no real way of getting them out of my head and into anyone else’s hands.

I waited far too long. Life got in the way, as it does. Bills. Work. Responsibilities. I have always had a full-time job, and I still do. The hours that were left over were never quite enough, and I could not afford the things a book is supposed to need. I could not pay artists for cover art. I could not pay editors to go through a manuscript. So the stories stayed where they had always lived, in the dark, after the lights went out, while I tried to get to sleep.

I am writing this because that has changed, and because I want other people to know it can change for them too.

I still write the majority of my books myself. Sometimes I sit down and type. Sometimes I dictate them as live recordings, talking the story out the way it comes, and then feed those recordings into a program that puts them together for me on paper, so to speak. That gives me a manuscript I can actually hold and read back. From there I hand it to AI to edit.

I want to be clear about that last part, because I think it matters. The AI does not invent the book. It does not dream up the Scrapyard, or the people on that train, or the dark corners of the other stories I have told. I do that. What it does is help me tidy the pages I could not have paid someone else to tidy. And even then, I do not take its word for it. I check every edit. I go through the whole thing with a fine-tooth comb, more than once, until I am happy with it. If a change does not sound like me, it does not stay.

That is the process behind the books I have been able to put out into the world: The Scrapyard and its sequel Terminus, A Stitch in the Dark, Antiseptic Illusion, and Jacob’s Rest. None of them would be sitting on a shelf, or on a Kindle, if I had waited until I could afford the traditional version of this path. I would still be the boy lying awake, and the man doing the same, with a head full of places nobody else had ever been allowed to visit.

I am not going to pretend this is the same as having a publisher, a copy-editor and a cover designer all in your corner. It isn’t. There are still rough edges, I am sure, and I am still learning. But it is a way through. For someone holding down a job, paying the bills, and trying to be a responsible adult in the hours that are left, it is the first way through I have ever had.

I am sure there are millions of people like me. People who have had brilliant ideas and whole worlds in their heads that have never seen the light of day. Not because the stories weren’t good enough, but because life is heavy, time is short, and money, confidence, and the feeling that you need permission have always stood in the way. For a long time I believed I had missed my chance. I thought the window had closed somewhere in all those years of work and worry.

It had not. It had just been waiting for tools I did not used to have.

If you have a story in you, I would like you to hear that. You do not need to wait until you can afford a studio of people, or until the day job somehow disappears. You can write it yourself, or speak it into a recorder on the walk home, or steal an hour before the house wakes up. You can use the programs that now exist to help you shape those pages, provided you stay honest about the work and you read every line yourself. Then you can put it out and see what happens.

That is all I have done. I waited far too long to do it. The boy who lay awake at night, and the man he became, never had the opportunity to share any of it until now. I do not want that to be true for you as well.

If you have been carrying a world around, start. Write a page. Record a chapter. Make a mess and then comb through it until it feels like yours. The tools are here, at last, to help you finish the job. The rest is courage, a bit of stubbornness, and the decision not to take those stories to the grave.

As a small and happy postscript, The Scrapyard has just received its first blogger review, from Terry Mitchell at I Speak Book Nerd, who gave it four out of five Nerds and called it “a quick, transporting read.” I am grateful for that, and still a little stunned that the world I used to turn over in the dark is now being turned over by someone else. That is the whole point of getting the work out of your head.

So if you have been waiting, the way I waited, I hope you will stop waiting. Start your own story. The worlds in our heads were never meant to stay there.
