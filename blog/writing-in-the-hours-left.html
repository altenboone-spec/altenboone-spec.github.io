Writing in the Hours Left

The day job pays the bills. The stories pay something else.

Two years in the RAF. Twenty-six as a salesman. The worlds in my head were never for sale — but they also weren’t going to write themselves while I waited for a perfect stretch of empty calendar. That stretch doesn’t arrive. You steal the hours instead.

Early mornings. Late nights. The odd lunch break with a notebook instead of a phone. None of it looks glamorous from the outside, and it shouldn’t. Craft is mostly showing up when you’re tired and still putting one true sentence after another. I’ve lost more scenes to exhaustion than to lack of ideas. The ideas have been stuck inside me since I was a boy. The hard part is defending the window long enough to let them out.

If you’re in the same boat — writing around shifts, kids, a commute that actually does end where it’s supposed to — protect the small blocks. Forty-five minutes is enough to move a chapter. A weekend afternoon is enough to finish a fight scene you’ve been circling for a week. Don’t wait for a sabbatical. Build the bookshelf one early morning at a time.

I’m working toward the day I get to do this full-time. Until then, the hours left are where the books live. One book at a time. That’s enough.
