The Turned King Is Coming

Book 3 of The Scrapyard Chronicles is finished, uploaded, and waiting at the gate.

Book three is finished. This week I uploaded the final manuscript of The Turned King — Book 3 of The Scrapyard Chronicles — to Amazon KDP. It’s sitting in review now, which takes as long as it takes, but the hard part is done. The words are down, the full stops are in, and the story is out of my head and into the world. Or nearly.

I wanted to write something here while it still feels fresh, because this one took a lot out of me.

Where we left them

If you’ve read Terminus, you know how that ended. The walls came down. The one place in the Scrapyard that felt like civilisation — a city with a Governor, an Inner District, rules and rations and a roof over your head — fell in a single night. The Turned King picks up in the ash straight after. Thirty-odd survivors crammed into a fissure in the rock thirteen miles east of the city, bleeding, starving, short on bullets and shorter on hope. Seekers with empty rifles. Farm labourers in mud-caked rags. Office workers who never expected to have to run for anything.

That’s the kind of start I like. No heroes on horseback. Just ordinary people who have lost everything and have to decide, again, whether they’re going to keep walking.

What the book is about

Matt, Amanda, Alice and the rest are on the run across the Wastes, and they are not running from nothing. Something has taken Terminus for its own, and it has a name now. The Turned King. I won’t say much more about him, except that he was the most interesting character I’ve ever had to sit down and write. He isn’t a monster in a cupboard. He thinks. He builds. And that’s worse.

Out in the violet ash, the survivors find shelter in places that make no sense — ancient black-glass structures left behind by someone who was here long before any of us, and a few strangers who know a lot more about the Scrapyard than they’re letting on. One of them is a gunslinger called Hall, who I’ll admit I had far too much fun with.

If I’m honest, Hall is my own personal Han Solo. Every series needs one: the scoundrel who swaggers in, talks a good game, is clearly hiding something, and you still can’t help liking. I didn’t plan it that way. He just turned up on the page with a gun on his hip and an answer for everything, and before long he had me grinning at the keyboard. I like to think that if the Scrapyard had a cantina, Hall would already be in the corner, boots up on the table, insisting he shot first.

And somewhere out past the ridges there’s another power entirely. If you’ve wondered what else the Scrapyard has swallowed over the centuries, the prologue answers some of that, and it’s not good news for anyone.

It’s a book about what’s left when the safe place is gone. About whether you can build a home again, or whether you just keep moving. About who you trust, and what it costs you when you get it wrong.

Where it takes the series

The first book threw people into the Scrapyard. The second gave them a city and then took it away. This third one blows the map wide open. We go further across the Wastes than we’ve ever gone, we start to learn who built some of the things scattered across that world, and a couple of mysteries that have been quietly ticking since Book 1 finally start to show their teeth.

It also leaves some doors open on purpose. The story is a long way from over. Book four is already rattling around in my head, and it’s going to pay off a lot of what’s set up here.

Writing it

I’ll be honest — some chapters in this one were hard to write. The Scrapyard has never been a kind place, but in The Turned King people get hurt, and some of them have to carry that hurt for the rest of the book. Amanda’s arm. The quiet in a cave full of strangers who all lost someone the night before. I didn’t want to skip past that. When the worst happens, people don’t just dust themselves off in the next chapter. They limp. They snap at each other. They make bad jokes because it’s that or crying.

And there are moments of warmth in among it, because there have to be. A hot meal from a machine nobody understands. A night round a fire where two species who can’t stand each other end up sharing a drink and regretting it in the morning. Those small things matter more in a place like the Scrapyard. They matter in ours too.

Like everything I write, this book was built in the hours left over — early mornings, late nights, a lot of rewriting. Three books in, I still don’t take it for granted that anyone wants to come along. Every reader who has picked up The Scrapyard or Terminus and asked “what happens next?” is the reason this one exists.

When can you read it?

Soon. KDP review can take a little while, but once The Turned King clears it’ll be up on Amazon, and I’ll post the link here and on the Books page the moment it goes live.

In the meantime, if you haven’t started the series, now’s the perfect time. Begin with Book 1, The Scrapyard, then catch up with Terminus before the King arrives. You can find everything on my Amazon author page.

And if you’ve already read them — thank you. A short review on Amazon makes a real difference to an independent author, and I read every one.

See you in the Scrapyard.
